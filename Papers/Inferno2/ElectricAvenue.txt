Yesterday I had a thought cross my mind... a 
rather intriguing one at that. I thought "hmm,
I what if I patch the PEB in just the right 
place so that the linked list would return to 
the head early".

Normally I just 0 out everything if I want to 
remove a single entry, but the thing is, it 
is pretty noticeable.

A linked list legit is just a circular list
that is walked by [head+0] aka .flink which
inevitably loops back around the the original
head per the term "circular"

Lets say we have 10 entries inside of a linked
list. Well I had a thought... what I swap the,
lets just say, 3rd entries .flink to point 
towards the head? Making walking the linked
list to only show 3 entries and then loop back.

Below I will provide some asm that does exactly 
that. It will walk the PEB and force the ldr list
to only have 3 entries no matter how many Dlls get 
loaded.

You can do this on whichever entry you want, but 
cutting it off after kernel32 just makes sense for
this example.

peb
mov rax, [rax+24]
mov rax, [rax+16]
mov rsi, rax            ; store head
mov rax, [rax]
mov rax, [rax]
mov [rax], rsi          ; patch .flink of kernel32
mov rax, [rax+48]       ; Return kernel32 Base
ret
END

If you run this in Zazm, or convert it to a different
assemblers format, then youll get a PIC blob that can
be ran anywhere in memory to patch all the loaded Dlls
of the current process. 

Im not going to be going into the upsides of this tech
because its pretty obvious why hiding loaded dlls is 
a good offensive move.

I will, however, be going over the bad and the ugly.

Obviously any DLL in memory, linked list or not, will
still be in memory somewhere around the common dll range
and will be a huge red flag to memory scanners.

Choose to use wisely.
