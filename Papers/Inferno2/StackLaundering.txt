

            Laundering Stacks
                 Sleepy


For nearly my entire lifetime, virus authors have been
abusing the stack.

Recently while building my assembler v2, I noticed something
I could pull off using just 2 instructions.

Normally when someing is reversing a binary, seeing something 
along the lines of:

mov rax, [rsp+8]

Would make someone say "Okay this person is fetching something 
from the stack." and allow them to set a breakpoint, dump the 
stack and check what is at rsp+8.

But what I thought was, what if I store rsp in a non volitile 
register or even rax before referncing its internals??

It will look like this:

sub rsp, 32
lea rcx, [P]        ; Loading "calc.exe" from P label
push rcx            ; push rcx onto stack so rsp+0 is pointer
mov rax, rsp        ; mov rsp pointer into rax        
mov rax, [rax]      ; this is rsp+0 mov which is our pointer
mov rax, [rax]      ; deref pointer which is now a string
pop rcx             ; pop it out
add rsp, 32        

There are 2 reasons why I like doing this...

1: stack access obfuscation
2: No SIB

Even though Zazm, my assembler is capable of handling SIB bytes
there is a funny story on how I even thought of this idea to 
begin with.

When building an assembler, you kinda just stumble onto things 
you havnt even thought of adding yet, which leads into this 
cycle of adding onto your assembler. I think of building an
assembler as a 1 instruction a day kind of thing.

Well there was a bug in my line parser for rsp movs. Everytime 
I did "mov rax, [rsp]" my assembler would crash and output 
"mov rax, rsp" because the +0 or no displacement caused my 
assembler to treat [rsp] as a normal mov, not a deref.

So to work around this I thought, let me try "mov rax, [rsp+0]"
and nope. The assembler still sees 0 as a NULL value. 

I ended up noticing that you can just store the rsp register
pointer in any register and still treat it like the stack,
but minus a SIB byte. So technically we are only increasing 
our binary size by +2 each stack reference. Normally +3 with 
mov rax, [rax], but because we ditch our SIB byte, we minus 
1.

Pretty Cool, personally if I was reversing a program and saw:

mov rax, [rsi] or mov rsi, [rsp]

Pretty Cool

Until Next Time...

-Sleepy
